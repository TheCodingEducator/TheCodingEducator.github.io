What each part tells you

PartWhy it matters for this unitIf many students miss it
A: MultiplyingEvaluating powers is repeated multiplication. #3 catches the most common mistake: 2³ = 6.Quick fact practice; write powers out the long way before using shortcuts.
B: Powers of 10Students met exponents with 10 in grade 5; build on it.Count the zeros: 10³ has 3 zeros. Start the unit there.
C: Patterns#8 and #9 are powers of 2 and 3. #10 going down past 1 leads to zero and negative exponents.Use the doubling cells picture: 2¹, 2², 2³…
D: FractionsNegative exponents are fractions: 2⁻² = ¼.Review unit fractions before Part C of the worksheet.

Reading the results: students who answer 6 on #3 will write 2³ = 6; start the unit by writing powers out as repeated multiplication. Students strong on #10 are ready for zero and negative exponents early. Compare the “How sure do you feel?” circles with the answers: a student who circles “I've got it” but misses the part is worth a quick conversation.
