Exponent Racer

A driving game where you steer between lanes and answer exponent math questions to refuel and score points. Controls: arrow keys or WASD to move, space or enter to confirm answers, mouse to click menu buttons.

Quick Teacher Tips

Key vocabulary

  • base: the number being multiplied; in 4³, the base is 4
  • exponent: how many times the base is used as a factor; in 4³, the exponent is 3
  • power: a base with an exponent, like 4³
  • product of powers: same base, multiply: add the exponents (2³ · 2⁴ = 2⁷)
  • quotient of powers: same base, divide: subtract the exponents (5⁶ ÷ 5² = 5⁴)
  • power of a power: multiply the exponents ((3²)⁴ = 3⁸)
  • zero exponent: any nonzero base to the zero power is 1 (7⁰ = 1)
  • negative exponent: means one over the positive power: 5⁻² = 1/5² = 1/25

Tips for using the game

Students race through the integer-exponent unit, choosing which skills to practice and answering each question with a lane change.

  • The math: six skills, from evaluating a power (grade 6) to the product rule, quotient rule, power of a power, and negative exponents (grade 8), such as 5−2 = 1/25.
  • Vocabulary: one skill asks students to circle the base, the exponent, or the whole power in an expression, so the terms are tied to the symbols.
  • Powers of ten: questions with a base of 10 offer only powers of ten as wrong answers, which trains students to count zeros carefully.
  • Real life: powers of ten write the distance to the moon and the width of a cell, and exponents also describe doubling bacteria, compound interest, and computer storage.
  • Speed and review: the question timer tightens with each correct answer to build fluency, and the game-over screen lists missed questions beside the correct answers.
