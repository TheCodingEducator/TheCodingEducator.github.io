Exponent Racer

A driving game where you steer between lanes and answer exponent math questions to refuel and score points. Controls: arrow keys or WASD to move, space or enter to confirm answers, mouse to click menu buttons.

📏 Common Core Standard

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.8.EE.A.1

Know and apply the properties of integer exponents to generate equivalent numerical expressions.

e.g. 3² × 3⁻⁵ = 3⁻³ = 1/3³ = 1/27

Teaching Notes for Educators

Exponent Racer is built the way I'd build a lesson - differentiated, low-stakes, and designed so a student's reading level never gets in the way of the math.

  • Differentiated by skill and by pace. Before each run, students choose exactly which of the 6 exponent skills to practice, and progress from Street Racing to Maximum Velocity mode as mastery builds. Inside a run, the question timer tightens a little with every correct answer, so the game keeps nudging a student toward faster recall instead of forcing everyone through the same fixed pace.
  • Rule-based feedback, not just right or wrong. Every missed question is followed by a plain-language explanation of the rule that applies (for example, "A negative exponent flips the base to the denominator"), so a mistake becomes a teaching moment instead of just a lower score.
  • Vocabulary built in. A dedicated skill has students identify the base, exponent, or power within an expression, reinforcing the academic vocabulary alongside the symbols themselves.
  • Language-light and ESL-friendly. Gameplay runs on numbers and symbols, not word problems - a student can play, understand their score, and read the answer choices with very little English reading required.
  • Low-stakes practice. No accounts, no logins, and no permanent record of mistakes. Students can retry as many times as they need without it feeling like a graded test.
  • Mistake review at the end of every run. The game-over screen lists recently missed questions side-by-side with the correct answer, giving students (and teachers glancing over a shoulder) a quick summary of what to revisit.
  • Works on any device. On-screen joystick and action-button controls appear automatically on phones and tablets, so it's just as playable on a school Chromebook as it is at home.
