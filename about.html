Hi, I'm TheCodingEducator

I'm an 8th grade math teacher. Like most teachers, I'm always looking for ways to make practice feel less like a worksheet and more like something students actually want to do.

Why I built this

I'd find great educational games online - the kind that make kids want to practice math - only to discover our school's internet filters blocked them. Most of those sites were never built with a classroom in mind: they were loaded with tracking, ads aimed at adults, or data collection that no school could responsibly allow in front of a room full of minors.

So I decided to build my own. This site is COPPA compliant from the ground up - no accounts, no logins, no personal data collected from students, and ads configured specifically so they're safe for young players. That's not an afterthought here; it's the whole reason this site exists. It means a teacher can pull it up on a classroom projector or a school computer without worrying it'll get blocked or bring up something inappropriate.

How I design the games

I'm not a professional game developer - I'm a teacher who happens to code. Every game here is built the way I'd build a lesson: with real students, and the range of levels they walk in at, in mind.

  • Differentiated by design. Students can choose exactly which skills to practice - whether that's the basics of evaluating a power, or trickier territory like negative exponents - so a student who needs to shore up fundamentals and a student ready to be pushed can both be working at the right level at the same time.
  • Built-in progression. Games start at an approachable difficulty and unlock a harder mode as students show they're ready, so the challenge grows with the student instead of overwhelming them on day one.
  • Fun first, because engagement is what makes practice stick. Racing, shooting hoops, dodging traffic - the math is the obstacle standing between a student and the fun part, which is exactly the kind of motivation that gets kids to do twenty more problems than they would on paper.
