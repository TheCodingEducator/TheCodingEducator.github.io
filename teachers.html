More for teachers

Ideas for using the games in class
  • Printables. Every game has a worksheet with an answer key and a two-to-a-page exit ticket; several also have a pre-assessment, visual notes and an entry ticket. Print a whole unit at once with its unit pack.
  • Warm-ups (5 minutes). Most games have a quick mode or a first level that fits the start of class.
  • Stations. One station plays while you work with a small group. Games that let students choose skills make this easy to differentiate.
  • Launching a lesson. Project a game and play it together before you name the rule. Tip the Scales' Sandbox and Pythagorean Platforms' World 1 work especially well for this.
  • Review days. Exponent Hoops, Linear World Cup and Let's Get to the Point have two-player modes for a friendly competition.
  • Connect back to paper. Ask students to write down each move or step the game asks for. The game becomes a worked solution in their notebook.

School filters and accessibility

Getting the games past school filters

This site was built so it can run on school networks. There are no accounts and no chat, and nothing students type is sent anywhere. Progress (like best times and coins) saves only in that browser on that device, and students can erase it any time with the Reset progress button.

If your school's filter blocks the site, ask your technology department to allow studentmathgames.com. It may also help to mention that the site keeps no student data, uses a cookie-free visitor counter, and that any ads are non-personalized because the site is registered with Google as directed to children. The privacy policy explains exactly what the site does and doesn't collect.

Accessibility

Every game can be played in Spanish with the 🌐 Español button in its top bar (the choice is remembered for all the games on that device). Every game can be played with the keyboard alone: arrow keys move between buttons, Enter or Space presses them. Games fill the screen on a computer, and on phones and tablets the page never scrolls. Read more on the accessibility page.
