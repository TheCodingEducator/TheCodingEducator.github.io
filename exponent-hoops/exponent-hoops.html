Exponent Hoops

A basketball shooting game where you solve exponent rules to score. Play solo against a computer defender, or head-to-head with a friend. Controls: WASD or arrow keys to move, space or enter to shoot or answer questions, mouse to click menu buttons.

📏 Common Core Standard

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.8.EE.A.1

Know and apply the properties of integer exponents to generate equivalent numerical expressions.

e.g. 3² × 3⁻⁵ = 3⁻³ = 1/3³ = 1/27

Teaching Notes for Educators

Exponent Hoops covers the same core exponent rules as Exponent Racer - multiplying powers, dividing powers, a power raised to a power, and the zero-exponent rule - but with variable bases (x, y, a, b) instead of just numbers, so it doubles as a bridge into algebraic exponent expressions. Every shot is a fresh application of one of those rules, solo against a defender or head-to-head against a friend.

  • Differentiated shot by shot. A 2-point shot asks an easier, positive-exponent question. Stepping back for a 3-pointer raises the stakes with a harder mixed question that can include negative exponents - so students choose their own challenge level in real time, every possession.
  • Rule-based feedback, every single shot. Whether a shot is made, missed, or blocked, the explanation screen shows the full equation and states the exponent rule used to solve it (for example, "Rule: Add exponents"), reinforcing why an answer is correct instead of just whether it was.
  • Icon-based answer choices. Solo-mode answers are shown with simple directional arrows instead of text labels, so students can play and read feedback without needing strong English reading skills.
  • Solo or versus. Play solo against a computer defender for self-paced, low-pressure practice, or head-to-head with a friend for competitive, motivating peer practice.
  • Low-stakes practice. No accounts, no logins, and no permanent record of mistakes. Students can play as many rounds as they need without it feeling like a graded test.
  • Works on any device. On-screen joystick and action-button controls appear automatically on phones and tablets, so it's just as playable on a school Chromebook as it is at home.
