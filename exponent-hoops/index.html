Exponent Hoops

A basketball shooting game where you solve exponent rules to score. Play solo against a computer defender, or head-to-head with a friend. Controls: WASD or arrow keys to move, space or enter to shoot or answer questions, mouse to click menu buttons.

📏 Common Core Standard

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.8.EE.A.1

Know and apply the properties of integer exponents to generate equivalent numerical expressions.

e.g. 3² × 3⁻⁵ = 3⁻³ = 1/3³ = 1/27

Quick Teacher Tips

Key vocabulary

  • base: the number or variable being multiplied; in x⁵, the base is x
  • exponent: how many times the base is used as a factor; in x⁵, the exponent is 5
  • power: an expression with a base and an exponent, like x⁵
  • product of powers: same base, multiply: add the exponents (x² · x⁴ = x⁶)
  • quotient of powers: same base, divide: subtract the exponents (x⁷ ÷ x³ = x⁴)
  • power of a power: multiply the exponents ((x²)³ = x⁶)
  • zero exponent: any nonzero base to the zero power is 1 (x⁰ = 1)

Tips for using the game

Students apply the exponent rules to variable bases (x, y, a, b) to score, which bridges number exponents and algebraic expressions.

  • The math: multiplying powers with the same base adds exponents, dividing subtracts them, a power raised to a power multiplies them, and any base to the zero power is 1.
  • Choose your challenge: a 2-point shot asks a positive-exponent question, and stepping back for a 3-pointer brings in harder mixed questions with negative exponents.
  • The rule is named every time: after each shot the full equation appears with the rule used, such as "Add exponents," so students learn why an answer works.
  • Real life: the same rules simplify formulas in algebra, physics, and computer science, and they are the foundation for polynomials and scientific notation later on.
  • Play styles: solo against a defender for quiet practice, or head-to-head with a friend for a review-day competition.
