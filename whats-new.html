What's New

The games on this site are updated constantly. Here is every new game and every notable change, newest first.

October 9, 2026

  • Every game now ends with results by skill: for each skill practiced, how many answers, how many right, and the percent, with a word and a color band (80% and up "Strong", 40–79% "Growing", under 40% "Keep practicing"). It appears at the end of a round, run, match, shift or world, or when leaving. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere. Battle on the Coordinate Plane's battle report now shows the same percent bands.
  • The For Teachers page is now organized by unit (angle relationships, transformations and similarity, the Pythagorean theorem, equations, exponents and roots, and more), and each unit lists its standards and all of its worksheets and exit tickets in one line.
  • My Stats: Laser Heist now shows the real numbers (stars, rooms cleared, diamonds, best streak, suits and lasers, and whether the Golden Angle is recovered), with a new "Golden Angle" badge. "Reset all progress" on My Stats now clears every game.
  • Pythagorean Platforms: screen readers now hear the explanation after a wrong step, and square roots in the answer choices are read as "square root of".
  • New game: Math Billiards (grade 8). A real top-down pool game where every shot is an angle problem. Aim the cue anywhere, and the game draws the geometry of that exact shot: a guide line, the cushion, or two parallel lines, with x to find. The right answer plays the shot as aimed; a wrong answer plays the shot that angle would really make, then shows both paths side by side and brings back the same shot with more help each time. Practice complementary, supplementary and vertical angles and parallel lines cut by a transversal at Beginner, Intermediate or Challenge level, in Practice mode, classroom 8-ball against the computer (it shows its angle work step by step), or two players on one device. Correct answers earn coins for cues, tables, ball styles and shot effects. With a teacher guide, a printable worksheet and an exit ticket.

October 8, 2026

  • Laser Heist: after getting caught three times and sent back to the questions, students need at least one more right answer before they can try the sneak again. Until then, the missed panels come back with new angles.

The real shot is rounded to the nearest 10° or 5°, and the ball is turned to match, so the picture and the path always agree.
  • Laser Heist: the Shop buttons on the tower map and the floor-cleared screens open the shop again (before, only the title screen's button worked).

  • Laser Heist: the Rail Yard and the Penthouse are now one floor. Floor 4 asks about all eight angles on parallel lines, and its last room, where the Golden Angle is kept, mixes every kind of angle. The Algebra Vault is now Floor 5. Saved stars carry over to the new rooms.
  • Laser Heist: each floor ends with a "what to review" card showing right and wrong answers for each kind of angle, with the ones to work on flagged. After two misses in a row on one kind of angle, the next question of that kind opens with a quick refresher of the rule.
  • Laser Heist: the first time through the Lobby, short prompts teach the sneak ("Stay out of the light," "Wait for the tripwire to go dark"), and the clock waits until you first move. When the lights are out, a faint arrow points toward the exit. Every room now keeps your best time, shown on its door.
  • Laser Heist worksheet: a new Part F matches Floor 4. One angle on parallel lines is given and x is any of the other seven; students color-code every angle equal to the given one in yellow and every angle equal to x in blue, with a colored answer key.
  • Laser Heist: every sneak now gives 20 seconds, and every room has been checked to be beatable with at least 3 seconds to spare, whatever the answers. Every room has at least one blinking tripwire that no panel switches off, placed on the way to the exit (some now stretch across a whole room); cameras never watch the exit; and guards' routes are no longer drawn.
  • Laser Heist: after a parallel-lines puzzle, every angle equal to the given one is labeled in its gold, and every angle equal to x in its blue. Security cameras also see a narrower, shorter cone, and the sneak clock is now 20 seconds.
  • Laser Heist, Floor 4: each puzzle now gives one angle on the parallel lines and asks for any of the other seven: corresponding, alternate interior or exterior, co-interior, same-side exterior, vertical, on a straight line, or two steps away. The panel names the pair and its rule each time, and Practice has a new "Parallel lines: any angle" choice.
  • Laser Heist: the "equal, or add to 180°?" question before each vertical and parallel-line puzzle is gone; students go straight to typing the angle.
  • Bank Shot: vertical angles are now part of the game in both modes. About half the straight shots draw two lines crossing at the ball (its path and a guide line), and students find the angle across from the given one.
  • Bank Shot: Hole-In-One Hero's algebra is simpler. On holes 7 to 9 the missing angle is written as (x + 5)° or (x − 3)°: find the angle, then undo the + 5 to get x. The hints name the two common slips (stopping at the angle, or undoing it the wrong way), and the worksheet and exit ticket now practice the same thing.
