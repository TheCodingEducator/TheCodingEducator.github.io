What's New

The games on this site are updated constantly. Here is every new game and every notable change, newest first.

October 4, 2026

  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: fixed the HOW TO PLAY button in the Esc menu, which opened a blank screen.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: the Sound on/off switch moved into the Esc menu, next to Less Motion.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: at 3-Star General, a turn now asks at most one question before the shot, and the clockwise or counterclockwise question is its own Turn Check about a scout plane.

  • Every game now works with screen readers (programs that read the screen aloud for blind and low-vision students): questions, answer choices, hints, feedback and results are read aloud as they appear, math like 34 is read as "3 to the power of 4," and icon-only buttons have spoken names. See the Accessibility page for details.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: the computer now plays at the level of each rank (checking around its hits at 1-Star and 2-Star General, and also following a ship at 3-Star; a 1-Star general sometimes forgets and wastes a shot on a point it already tried). After the first three shots, the plane flies 50% faster (back to normal for one flight after a wrong answer). The 3-Star timer is now 30 seconds, every Radar Check always offers a full set of choices on the smaller board, a few quiet sounds (the enemy-fire siren, firing, lock-on and thunder) are louder, and screen readers can now hear each board's full shot history and where your ships are.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: every screen now fits in the game window with nothing to scroll, on computers, tablets and phones (upright or sideways). The boards are sized to fit, and long screens like the instructions shrink to fit. The on-screen number pad now offers only the numbers that are on the board.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: smaller boards of 25 points in every mode: 0 to 4 for 1-Star General, and −2 to 2 for 2-Star and 3-Star General. The grid spacing is wider, so the boards are just as big on screen.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: each rank now has its own boards. 2-Star General boards sit in a riveted steel ship's-deck frame with brass nameplates; 3-Star General boards are a glowing command-center radar console with targeting brackets, a radar sweep and hazard-striped nameplates. The coordinate grids stay clean and easy to read.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: while placing the fleet, ships can now be moved or rotated on top of each other. Overlapping ships turn red, the shared points are circled in red, and START GAME stays locked until the ships are on different points. (START GAME now also looks greyed out whenever it can't be pressed.)
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: the turn question's explanation now speaks only in clockwise and counterclockwise (no "turn left" or "turn right"), and adds the pattern: Quadrants I and III always turn counterclockwise, Quadrants II and IV always turn clockwise.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: the three modes are now ranks: 1-Star General (first quadrant), 2-Star General (all four quadrants) and 3-Star General (Coordinate Commander), each shown with silver general's stars on a shoulder board. The game also has all-new sound effects of its own, like a ship's bell for correct answers and a foghorn when time runs out.
  • Arrow keys now work in every game's menus after pressing Esc (pause menus, "leave this game?" boxes, end screens and level pickers), including Up and Down on boxes whose buttons sit side by side. If nothing is selected yet, the first arrow press jumps into the open box.
  • Battle on the Coordinate Plane: the ship panel no longer jumps around while ships are moved (the coordinates and messages keep a fixed size), and the clockwise or counterclockwise turn question now appears only in Hard mode.
  • Quick Teacher Tips on every game is now titled Quick Teacher Tips and opens with the Key vocabulary, followed by the tips for using the game.
  • Every game's Full Teacher Guide and Quick Teacher Tips now include a Key vocabulary section: the important math words and phrases for that game (like ordered pair, quadrant, hypotenuse, unit rate or inverse operations), each with a short, student-friendly definition and example. The printable worksheets now have the same words in a Key vocabulary box at the top.
  • New link-preview pictures: when you share any game, guide, worksheet or exit ticket in a message, email or post, the preview now shows that game's own picture with the studentmathgames.com name. Area Artist, Battle on the Coordinate Plane, Proportional Plates, Pythagorean Platforms and Rooted to the Spot have pictures for the first time.
  • Privacy Policy updated: it now explains the privacy-friendly visitor counter (no cookies, no personal information), Google AdSense ads (non-personalized, because the site is tagged as directed to children under COPPA, with a consent choice for visitors in Europe), and the fonts and game files loaded from Google Fonts and cdnjs. The About, For Teachers and Terms pages now say the same.

  • New game: Battle on the Coordinate Plane (grades 5–6). A sea battle against the computer where every shot is an ordered pair. Students hide two ships on the points of their own coordinate plane, type coordinates to fire, and watch a plane fly from the origin left or right for x, then up or down for y. On the computer's turn they must find its shot on their own board, with feedback that names the mistake (x and y swapped, wrong direction) and stronger hints each try. Three modes: Easy (first quadrant, 0 to 6), Standard (all four quadrants, −3 to 3) and Hard (a timer, typed ordered pairs, shots described in words with y sometimes first, and quick quadrant questions). Spanish, keyboard-only play, a teacher guide, a worksheet and an exit ticket are included.

  • Bank Shot: new look for wrong answers. A small card at the top adds the two angles and shows they miss 180° (or 90°), without giving away the answer.
  • Bank Shot: after a wrong answer, the red shot line now stops at the wall too, just like the green line for a right answer.

September 30, 2026

  • Bank Shot: the shot lines are always straight. Water currents, hills and bushes can still steer the ball, but the drawn angle lines no longer bend with them.
  • Bank Shot: after a wrong answer the ball rolls a little slower (same path), so it's easier to watch where the typed angle sends it.
  • Bank Shot: the round bushes on the course are smaller, leaving more room to play around them.
  • Bank Shot: wrong answers now play out the student's own number. On a wall shot the ball rolls to the wall and leaves it at the typed angle (shown with a red arc, without giving away the correct answer); on a straight shot it rolls along the typed angle. When the ball stops, the explanation card opens, and then the same question is asked again with a hint for that question: supplementary angles add up to 180° (wall shots) or complementary angles add up to 90° (straight shots).
  • Bank Shot: the question diagram has just one white dotted line, the ball's path, and the two colored angles meet exactly on it.
  • Bank Shot: on every shot, the green line now follows the ball and stops the moment it touches a rail (straight shots no longer cut off early).
  • Bank Shot: after a shot, the green line follows the ball's real path exactly and lies right on top of the white dotted line to the wall.
  • Bank Shot: the green shot line no longer runs through rails. It stops at the first rail in its way.
  • Bank Shot: on straight shots, the missing complementary angle can now be any whole number from 1° to 89°, on every hole.
  • Bank Shot: the angle question now always shows up where you aimed. A ball starting right next to a rail no longer counts that rail as hit, and a shot a bush or slope would steer into some other wall now gets the straight-shot question instead.
  • Bank Shot: a shot at a wall now asks for the supplementary angle (the ball's path and the wall make a straight line, 180°), and a straight shot into open green asks for the complementary angle (a right angle with the aim line as one side, 90°). The teacher guide's worked examples match.
  • Bank Shot: after a shot, the game shows the exact angle diagram from the question, with the "?" filled in by the answer (green, or red when the answer was wrong).
  • Bank Shot: the green shot line now sits exactly on the angle diagram: a wall shot comes in along the line between the two angles and stops at the wall; a straight shot runs along the side of the right angle. Both angles stay visible as the shot is hit.
  • Bank Shot: in a wall question, the ball's white dotted path now runs right to the wall and is the line between the two angles.
  • Bank Shot: after a shot, the green line now stops when the ball hits a second wall.
