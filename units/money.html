Answer Key: Counting Coins

For the teacher
  1. 25, 50, 60, 61, 62, 63 → 63¢
  2. 10, 20, 30, 35, 40 → 40¢
  3. 25, 30, 35, 40, 45 → 45¢
  4. 25, 50, 75, 100, 105 → $1.05
  5. For example: 1 quarter, 1 dime, 1 nickel, 1 penny (25 + 10 + 5 + 1)
  6. 2 quarters and 1 dime (25 + 25 + 10). Another way: 6 dimes.
  7. 2 coins: 1 quarter and 1 nickel
  8. For example: 3 quarters; or 7 dimes and 1 nickel; or 2 quarters, 2 dimes and 1 nickel
  9. 25 + 25 + 10 + 10 → 70¢
  10. 50 − 35 → 15¢
  11. 80 − 45 → 35¢
  12. Sam counted the coins instead of their values. 10 + 10 + 10 + 5 → 35¢
  13. $0.75
  14. 135¢

Have students say the running total out loud with each coin (25, 50, 60…). In the game, the piggy bank changes color as the total gets close, which helps students slow down before they go over.

Counting Coins
  1. 25, 35, 45, 46, 47 → 47¢
