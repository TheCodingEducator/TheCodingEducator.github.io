Visual Notes: Ratios and Proportions

Grades 6–7 · CCSS 7.RP.A.2 · Pairs with the Proportional Plates game
Name

The big idea: when you make more (or less) of a recipe, every amount changes by the same factor. Triple the pancakes, triple the flour, triple the milk. That's a proportional relationship. Multiply; never add the difference.

Key vocabulary

Ratio

A comparison of two amounts: 2 : 4, 2 to 4, or 2/4.

Equivalent ratios

flour26
pancakes412

Ratios that make the same comparison: 2 : 4 = 6 : 12.

Rate

A ratio of two different units: $6 for 3 drinks.

Unit rate

The amount for 1: $6 ÷ 3 = $2 per drink.

Proportional relationship

Two amounts that always change by the same factor. Its graph is a straight line through 0.

Scale factor

4 × 3 → 12 pancakes

What every amount is multiplied by: 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

Constant of proportionality

y = kx

The unit rate k: $2 per drink means cost = 2 × drinks.

Two ways to solve a proportion (4 pancakes use 2 cups of flour. How much for 12?)

Method 1: Scale factor
pancakes4× 3 →12
flour2× 3 →6
12 ÷ 4 = 3, so multiply the flour by 3 too: 6 cups.
Method 2: Unit rate
pancakes4112
flour2½6
Flour for 1 pancake: 2 ÷ 4 = ½ cup. Then ½ × 12 = 6 cups.

Solved Step by Step: In the Kitchen and at the Market

Visual Notes: Ratios and Proportions, page 2

1. Scale up a recipe

3 cups of flour make 12 cookies. How much flour for 36 cookies?

  1. Scale factor: 36 ÷ 12 = 3
  2. Multiply: 3 × 3 cups = 9 cups
✓ Check: 9 : 36 simplifies to 3 : 12

2. A fraction scale factor

4 lemons make 6 cups. How many lemons for 9 cups?

  1. Scale factor: 9 ÷ 6 = 1½
  2. Multiply: 4 × 1½ = 6 lemons
✓ Unit rate check: ⅔ lemon per cup × 9 = 6

3. The better deal

16 oz of cereal for $4.00, or 24 oz for $5.40?

  1. Unit price A: $4.00 ÷ 16 = $0.25 per oz
  2. Unit price B: $5.40 ÷ 24 = $0.225 per oz
  3. B costs less per ounce: 24 oz is the better deal.
✓ Compare unit prices, not total prices

4. Is it proportional?

servings x246
cups y369
  1. Divide each y by x: 3/2, 6/4, 9/6 all = 1.5
  2. Same every time, so yes: y = 1.5x
✓ The graph is a straight line through (0, 0)
Watch out
  • Never add the difference. 4 pancakes use 2 cups; 12 pancakes is 8 more, but you need 6 cups, not 10.
  • Keep units lined up: pancakes over pancakes, flour over flour.
  • For “better deal” problems, find the price of one unit for each choice.

Proportions in the Kitchen

Grades 6–7 · CCSS 7.RP.A.2 · Pairs with the Proportional Plates game
Name
Date
In a proportional relationship, every amount changes by the same factor. Two ways to solve: find the scale factor (order ÷ recipe) and multiply, or find the unit rate (the amount for 1) and multiply. Never add the difference.

Key vocabulary

  • ratio: a comparison of two amounts, like 2 cups of flour for 4 pancakes (2 : 4)
  • equivalent ratios: ratios that make the same comparison, like 2 : 4 and 6 : 12
  • rate: a ratio comparing two different units, like $6 for 3 drinks
  • unit rate: the amount for 1 of something, like $2 per drink
  • proportional relationship: two amounts that always change by the same factor
  • scale factor: the number every amount is multiplied by (12 pancakes ÷ 4 pancakes = 3)
  • constant of proportionality: the unit rate in y = kx; the k that every input is multiplied by

Answer Key: Proportions in the Kitchen

For the teacher
  1. k = 12 / 4 = 3; 2 × 3 = 6 cups
  2. k = 8 / 2 = 4; 3 × 4 = 12 eggs
  3. k = 15 / 5 = 3; 10 × 3 = 30 oz
  4. k = 3 / 6 = ½; 4 × ½ = 2 cups
  5. Halve: 3 cookies → 2 eggs. Add columns 6 + 3: 9 cookies → 6 eggs
  6. 2 servings → 3; 6 servings → 9; 10 servings → 15 cups (1.5 cups per serving)
  7. $2.50 ÷ 5 = $0.50 each; 8 × $0.50 = $4.00
  8. $4.50 ÷ 3 = $1.50/lb; 7 × $1.50 = $10.50
  9. 12 oz: $3.00 ÷ 12 = $0.25/oz. 20 oz: $4.40 ÷ 20 = $0.22/oz. The 20 oz bag is the better deal.
  10. k = 6 / 4 = 1½; 2 × 1½ = 3 cups
  11. k = 4 / 6 = ⅔; 9 × ⅔ = 6 oz
  12. Casey added instead of multiplying. 12 pancakes is 3 times as many, so every ingredient triples: 2 × 3 = 6 cups

#12 is the most common proportion mistake. In the game, it's named in a pop-up with a tape diagram that shows why every amount has to grow by the same factor.
