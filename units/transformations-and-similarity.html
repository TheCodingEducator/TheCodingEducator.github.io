Visual Notes: Transformations

Grade 8 · CCSS 8.G.A.1 · Pairs with the Let's Get to the Point game
Name

The big idea: a transformation moves a shape. A translation slides it, a reflection flips it, and a rotation turns it. None of them change its size or shape, so the new shape is congruent to the old one. On a grid, each move has a rule for the coordinates.

Key vocabulary (red = before, the pre-image; blue = after, the image)

Transformation

A rule that moves or changes a figure on the grid.

Pre-image / image

The figure before (A) and after (A′, “A prime”) a move.

Translation

A slide: every point moves the same way. Here (x − 4, y − 3).

Reflection

A flip over a line. Over the y-axis: (x, y) → (−x, y).

Rotation

A turn around a point. 90° counterclockwise: (x, y) → (−y, x).

Rigid motion

A move that keeps size and shape: translations, reflections and rotations.

Congruent

Exactly the same size and shape, even if turned or flipped.

Every coordinate rule (each one moves P(3, 1) to P′)

Translation(x + a, y + b). Here (x − 2, y + 2)
Reflect over the x-axis(x, y) → (x, −y)
Reflect over the y-axis(x, y) → (−x, y)
Rotate 90° counterclockwise(x, y) → (−y, x)
Rotate 180°(x, y) → (−x, −y)
Rotate 90° clockwise(x, y) → (y, −x)

Solved Step by Step: Slides, Flips and Turns

Visual Notes: Transformations, page 2

1. Translate (x + 5, y − 2)

  1. Add 5 to each x: 5 right. Subtract 2 from each y: 2 down.
  2. A(−4, 1) → A′(1, −1)
  3. B(−2, 1) → B′(3, −1); C(−4, 4) → C′(1, 2)
✓ Check: every point moved the same way

2. Reflect over the x-axis

  1. Flipping over the x-axis keeps x and changes the sign of y.
  2. (1, 2) → (1, −2); (4, 2) → (4, −2)
  3. (1, 4) → (1, −4)
✓ Check: each point is the same distance from the x-axis, on the other side

3. Rotate 90° counterclockwise

  1. The rule: (x, y) → (−y, x). Swap, then make the new x its opposite.
  2. (2, 1) → (−1, 2); (4, 1) → (−1, 4)
  3. (4, 3) → (−3, 4)
✓ Check: the triangle turned a quarter turn, from quadrant I into quadrant II

4. Two moves in a row

Translate P(2, −3) by (x − 4, y + 1), then reflect over the x-axis.

  1. Translate: (2 − 4, −3 + 1) = P′(−2, −2)
  2. Reflect over the x-axis: P″(−2, 2)
✓ Check: do the moves in order; the second starts from P′
Watch out
  • Reflecting over the x-axis changes y (the point flips up or down), and over the y-axis changes x.
  • Counterclockwise is the opposite of the way clock hands move. 270° counterclockwise = 90° clockwise.
  • Label images with primes: A → A′ → A″.

Visual Notes: Similar Figures and Scale Factor

Grades 7–8 · CCSS 7.G.A.1, 8.G.A.4 · Pairs with the Similarity Builder game
Name

The big idea: similar figures are the same shape but not always the same size, like a photo and its enlargement. Every length is multiplied by the same number, the scale factor k, and every angle stays the same. Multiply to grow or shrink; never add.

Key vocabulary

Similar figures

Same shape: equal angles and proportional sides.

Dilation

A transformation that enlarges or shrinks a figure by a scale factor.

Scale factor (k)

k = image side ÷ original side

For 2 × 3 → 4 × 6: k = 4 ÷ 2 = 2.

Corresponding sides

Sides in matching positions (same color). Each pair has the same ratio, here 2.

Corresponding angles

Angles in matching positions. In similar figures they are equal.

Proportional

small34
big68

Every pair of matching sides has the same ratio: 6/3 = 8/4 = 2.

Grow, shrink or stay the same?

k > 1: enlargementThe image is bigger. (k = 2)
k < 1: reductionThe image is smaller. (k = ½)
k = 1: same sizeThe figures are congruent.
Two ways to use k: image side = k × original side, so original side = image side ÷ k. Always match sides in the same position before you divide.

Visual Notes: Similar Figures and Scale Factor, page 2

1. Find the scale factor

  1. Match sides: 3 → 9 and 5 → 15.
  2. k = image ÷ original = 9 ÷ 3 = 3
  3. It's more than 1, so it's an enlargement.
✓ Check: the other pair agrees, 15 ÷ 5 = 3

2. Use the scale factor

k = 2.5. The original side is 6. Find the image side.

  1. Image side = k × original side
  2. = 2.5 × 6 = 15
✓ Check: the other side, 4 × 2.5 = 10, matches the picture

3. Find k first, then the missing side

  1. Matching widths: 6 → 4, so k = 4 ÷ 6 = ⅔ (a reduction).
  2. Missing side = ⅔ × 9 = 6
✓ Check: 4/6 = 6/9 = ⅔

4. Real life: shadows

A 6 ft person casts a 4 ft shadow. At the same time a tree casts an 18 ft shadow. How tall is the tree?

  1. The sun makes similar triangles: k = 18 ÷ 4 = 4.5
  2. Tree = 4.5 × 6 = 27 ft
✓ Check: 6/4 = 27/18 = 1.5
Watch out
  • Multiply, don't add. If 3 grows to 9 (× 3), then 4 grows to 12, not 4 + 6 = 10.
  • Divide image by original (new ÷ old) to get k. Upside down gives the wrong k.
  • Angles never get multiplied. A 40° angle is still 40° after any dilation.
