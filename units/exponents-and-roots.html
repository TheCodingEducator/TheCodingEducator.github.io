Visual Notes: Exponents and the Exponent Rules

Grades 6–8 · CCSS 6.EE.A.1, 8.EE.A.1 · Pairs with the Exponent Racer game
Name

The big idea: an exponent says how many times to use a number as a factor. 2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. Every exponent rule comes from writing the factors out and counting them. Forget a rule? Write it out the long way and count.

Key vocabulary

Base

43

The number being multiplied: 4.

Exponent

43

How many times the base is a factor: 3.

Power

4³ = 4·4·4 = 64

A base with an exponent, and its value.

Product of powers

2³·2² = (2·2·2)(2·2) = 2⁵

Same base, multiply → add the exponents.

Quotient of powers

5⁴ ÷ 5² = 5·5·5·5⁄5·5 = 5²

Same base, divide → subtract the exponents.

Power of a power

(3²)³ = 3²·3²·3² = 3⁶

Multiply the exponents.

Zero exponent

7⁰ = 1

Any nonzero base to the 0 power is 1.

Negative exponent

5⁻² = 1⁄5² = 1⁄25

One over the positive power.

Why a⁰ = 1 and negatives make fractions: follow the pattern

2³2²2¹2⁰2⁻¹2⁻²
8421½¼
← each step to the right: ÷ 2 ←

Each time the exponent goes down by 1, the value is divided by 2. Keep going past 2¹ = 2: the next value is 1, so 2⁰ = 1. Then ½, then ¼: negative exponents make fractions, not negative numbers.

1. Evaluate a power

  1. Write it out: 3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3
  2. Multiply in steps: 3 × 3 = 9, 9 × 3 = 27, 27 × 3 = 81
✓ Not 3 × 4 = 12: the exponent counts factors
5² × 5³ = ?

2. Multiply powers

  1. Same base (5)? Yes, so add the exponents: 2 + 3 = 5
  2. 5² × 5³ = 5⁵
  3. Long way: (5·5)(5·5·5): five 5s
✓ 5⁵ = 3,125
(2²)³ = ?

3. Power of a power

  1. Multiply the exponents: 2 × 3 = 6
  2. (2²)³ = 2⁶ = 64
  3. Long way: 2² · 2² · 2² = 4 · 4 · 4 = 64
✓ Not 2⁵: that would be adding
4³ ÷ 4⁵ = ?

4. A negative exponent

  1. Divide → subtract: 3 − 5 = −2, so 4⁻²
  2. Make it positive: 4⁻² = 1⁄4² = 1⁄16
✓ Long way: two 4s are left on the bottom
Watch out
  • 2³ means 2 × 2 × 2 = 8, not 2 × 3 = 6.
  • The rules only work with the same base: 2³ × 5² can't be combined into one power.
  • A negative exponent makes a fraction, not a negative number: 2⁻³ = ⅛.

The big idea: x⁵ means x · x · x · x · x: five x's multiplied. The exponent rules are just counting x's. Multiply and the x's pile up (add). Divide and they cancel (subtract). Raise a power to a power and you copy the group (multiply).

Key vocabulary

Base

x5

The number or variable being multiplied: x.

Exponent

x5

How many times the base is a factor: 5.

Power

x³ = x·x·x

A base with an exponent.

Product of powers

x²·x³ = (x·x)(x·x·x) = x⁵

Same base, multiply: add exponents.

Quotient of powers

x⁵ ÷ x² = x·x·x·x·x⁄x·x = x³

Same base, divide: subtract exponents.

Power of a power

x⁰ = 1

Visual Notes: Square Roots and Cube Roots

Grade 8 · CCSS 8.EE.A.2, 8.NS.A.2 · Pairs with the Rooted to the Spot game
Name

The big idea: squaring a number makes a square: a side of 7 makes an area of 49. A square root goes backward: from an area of 49 back to a side of 7. A cube root does the same for a cube: from a volume back to its edge.

Key vocabulary

Perfect square

A whole number times itself: 4 × 4 = 16.

Square root (√)

√49 = 7

The number that, times itself, gives the original: 7 × 7 = 49.

Perfect cube

A whole number used 3 times as a factor: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.

Cube root (∛)

∛64 = 4

The number used 3 times to make it: 4 × 4 × 4 = 64.

Estimating a square root that isn't whole

To estimate √30: find the perfect squares on each side. 25 < 30 < 36, so 5 < √30 < 6. 30 is closer to 25 than to 36, so √30 is closer to 5 (about 5.5).
Square root vs. half: √36 is 6 (because 6 × 6 = 36), not 18. Square roots of numbers between 0 and 100 are always between 0 and 10.

  2. 5 × 5 × 5 = 125, so the edge is 5 in

  2. So 7 < √50 < 8
  3. 50 is just past 49, so √50 is just over 7 (about 7.1)

4. Rational or irrational?

  1. √16 = 4: a whole number, rational

✓ Irrational numbers still have a spot on the number line
