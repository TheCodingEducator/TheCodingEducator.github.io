Pythagorean Platforms

A jungle game about the Pythagorean theorem. Each canyon is a right triangle: leg a is the distance straight across to the next platform, leg b is how high the vine's anchor is above you, and the hypotenuse c is your rope. Solve one step of the Pythagorean theorem at a time by choosing the correct line of the written solution (tap it, or press the left or right arrow key); each correct step swings you to the next platform, and a wrong step drops you in the quicksand with a hint. The last step is the big swing: time slows down for a close-up that labels all three sides and reveals the missing length, and you land one rope length below the anchor, so short ropes keep you high in the trees and long ropes swing you low. World 1 walks through every step with tile squares, World 2 estimates square roots that are not whole numbers, and World 3 finds a missing leg, the distance to the next platform, from numbers carved on a temple door.

Pythagorean Platforms

How to play

  • One step per swing. Tap the next line of the written solution (or press the left or right arrow key). The right line swings you to the next platform.
  • Steps: pick the formula, plug in the legs, square, add, take the square root.
  • Each canyon is a right triangle: a is the distance across to the next platform, b is how high the vine's anchor is above you, and c is your rope. Find the rope length or the distance to the next platform.
  • When the problem is solved, time slows down for a close-up of all three sides, and then you take the big swing. The next platform is one rope length under the anchor, so a short rope keeps you high in the trees and a long rope swings you low.
  • A wrong line drops you in the quicksand, shows a hint, and you try that step again.

Why squares?

  • a² + b² = c² is about areas: the squares on the two legs exactly fill the square on the hypotenuse. Watch the squares on the canyon change as you solve.
  • Each wrong choice is a common mistake, and each one has its own hint.
  • Worlds 2 and 3 start with the formula already chosen. World 2 adds estimation. World 3 finds a missing leg: the numbers are carved on a temple door, and solving opens it.

    Teaching Notes for Educators

    Students swing across a jungle by solving the Pythagorean theorem one written step at a time. Every canyon is a right triangle made from the scene itself, so the numbers always describe something they can see.

    • The triangle: leg a (coral) is the distance straight across to the next platform, leg b (green) is how high the vine's anchor is above the explorer, and the hypotenuse c (blue) is the rope. The same three colors are used for every number on screen.
    • One step per swing: students choose the right line of the written solution - plug in, square, add (or subtract), take the square root - and each correct line swings them forward. Each wrong choice is a common mistake with its own hint, such as adding the sides, doubling instead of squaring, or forgetting the square root.
    • Why squares: a square is built on each side of the canyon triangle. As students work, the squares on the two legs fill with tiles and pour into the square on the rope, which they fill exactly - a visual proof of a² + b² = c².
    • Answers you can see: the big swing lands one rope length below the anchor, drawn to scale, so short ropes keep the explorer high in the trees and long ropes swing low. After the final step, a slow-motion close-up labels all three sides and reveals the missing length.
    • Three worlds: World 1 walks through every step with 3-4-5 triangles and unit tiles. World 2 gives the formula and adds estimating square roots that are not whole numbers. World 3 finds a missing leg - the distance to the next platform - from numbers carved on a temple door.
