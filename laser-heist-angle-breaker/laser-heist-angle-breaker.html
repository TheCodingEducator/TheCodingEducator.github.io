Laser Heist: Angle Breaker

A heist game where you solve supplementary, complementary, vertical, and parallel-lines-with-a-transversal angle puzzles to crack each room's security system, then sneak the robber past roaming patrol guards through a maze to reach the exit. Play the 5-sector Campaign, endless Challenge mode, or no-pressure Practice mode. Controls: number keys to type your answer, Enter to submit, arrow keys or WASD to move the robber during a sneak.

Teaching Notes for Educators

Laser Heist covers the full angle-relationships unit - supplementary and complementary pairs, vertical angles formed by two crossing lines, and the full set of corresponding/alternate/co-interior relationships created when a transversal cuts two parallel lines - by making each relationship the literal security system standing between the crew and the vault. A correct angle isn't just a point scored; it's translated directly into a real illuminated cone the student then has to physically dodge, so the geometry has an immediate, visible consequence instead of just a checkmark.

  • One relationship per sector, building in difficulty. Laser Grid (complementary) → Watch Team (supplementary) → Camera Crossfire (vertical) → Duct Crawl (parallel lines + transversal) → a mixed Vault Core boss round, matching the natural teaching progression from a single right-angle split up to the full eight-angle transversal figure.
  • The solved angle becomes a real, dodgeable hazard. Answer correctly and the exact wedge you just calculated is cast as an actual camera cone in a Pac-Man-style maze - a tangible payoff that ties the abstract "danger zone" of a geometry diagram to something the student has to actually navigate around.
  • Every relationship is labeled by name, not just solved. Each puzzle displays which relationship is in play (e.g. "Alternate Interior Angles," "Co-Interior Angles (Same-Side Interior)"), reinforcing the vocabulary alongside the calculation instead of treating every problem as an undifferentiated "find x."
  • Proof, not just claims. Any angle that lands on exactly 90° is marked with the standard geometric right-angle square, so "these are complementary because they sum to 90" is something a student can visually verify, not just trust.
  • A frozen clock protects a second attempt. A wrong answer doesn't reveal the solution or move on - it retries the identical puzzle with the timer stopped, so a student gets one genuine unpressured second look before the room changes.
  • Practice mode isolates exactly what a student needs. Check off any combination of the four relationship types for untimed, no-stakes, immediate-feedback repetition - useful for review sessions targeting one specific weak spot rather than the full campaign sequence.
  • Low-stakes overall. No accounts, and Practice mode in particular carries no score, timer, or lives at all - just reps.
