Penny = 1¢   Nickel = 5¢   Dime = 10¢   Quarter = 25¢  ·  Start with the biggest coins and count up. 100¢ = $1.00

Key vocabulary

  • penny: 1 cent (1¢)
  • nickel: 5 cents (5¢)
  • dime: 10 cents (10¢)
  • quarter: 25 cents (25¢)
  • cent (¢): the smallest unit of money; 100 cents make 1 dollar
  • dollar ($): 100 cents, written $1.00
  • value: how much a coin or a group of coins is worth
  • total: the amount you get when you add all the coins
  • skip counting: counting by the same amount each time: 5, 10, 15 or 25, 50, 75
  • decimal point: the dot in $1.35 that separates dollars from cents

Answer Key: Counting Coins

For the teacher
  1. 25, 50, 60, 61, 62, 63 → 63¢
  2. 10, 20, 30, 35, 40 → 40¢
  3. 25, 30, 35, 40, 45 → 45¢
  4. 25, 50, 75, 100, 105 → $1.05
  5. For example: 1 quarter, 1 dime, 1 nickel, 1 penny (25 + 10 + 5 + 1)
  6. 2 quarters and 1 dime (25 + 25 + 10). Another way: 6 dimes.
  7. 2 coins: 1 quarter and 1 nickel
  8. For example: 3 quarters; or 7 dimes and 1 nickel; or 2 quarters, 2 dimes and 1 nickel
  9. 25 + 25 + 10 + 10 → 70¢
  10. 50 − 35 → 15¢
  11. 80 − 45 → 35¢
  12. Sam counted the coins instead of their values. 10 + 10 + 10 + 5 → 35¢
  13. $0.75
  14. 135¢

Have students say the running total out loud with each coin (25, 50, 60…). In the game, the piggy bank changes color as the total gets close, which helps students slow down before they go over.
