What each part tells you

PartWhy it matters for this unitIf many students miss it
A: Count onAdding pennies is counting on by 1s, often past a decade (49, 50).Hundreds-chart practice crossing tens.
B: Skip countNickels count by 5s, dimes by 10s. #4 is counting on with dimes after a quarter.Skip-count chants with real or play coins.
C: Tens and ones62¢ is 6 dimes and 2 pennies: money is place value.Trade 10 pennies for a dime with play money.
D: AddEvery money total is an addition problem.Fact practice within 20 and adding tens.
E: CoinsStudents must name a coin before they can count it.Coin sorting with real coins; the visual notes show each coin's value.

Reading the results: students who skip count by 5s and 10s (Part B) are ready to count nickels and dimes on day one; students who mix up the dime and the nickel (the dime is smaller but worth more) need extra coin sorting. Compare the faces they circled with their answers.
