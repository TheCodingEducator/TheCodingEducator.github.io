Visual Notes: Counting Money

Grade 2 · CCSS 2.MD.C.8 · Pairs with the Piggy Bank Math game
Name

The big idea: each coin is worth a different number of cents. To count money, start with the coins worth the most, then count on. 100 cents make one dollar.

Key words

Penny

Worth 1 cent (1¢). Count by 1s.

Nickel

Worth 5 cents (5¢). Count by 5s.

Dime

Worth 10 cents (10¢). Count by 10s. It's small, but worth more than a nickel!

Quarter

Worth 25 cents (25¢). Count by 25s: 25, 50, 75, 100.

Cent (¢)

45¢

The unit for small amounts of money. 45¢ means 45 cents.

Dollar ($)

$1 = 100¢

A dollar is 100 cents, like 4 quarters or 10 dimes.

How to count coins

1. SortPut the coins in groups: quarters, dimes, nickels, pennies.
2. Biggest firstStart with the coins worth the most.
3. Count onAdd each coin's value as you go: 25, 35, 40, 41.
4. Write itUse ¢ for cents, or $ with a dot: 41¢ = $0.41.

Solved Step by Step

Visual Notes: Counting Money, page 2

1. Count a handful

  1. Quarters: 25, 50
  2. Dime: 60
  3. Pennies: 61, 62, 63
✓ Total: 63¢

2. Dimes and nickels

  1. Dimes by 10s: 10, 20, 30
  2. Nickels by 5s: 35, 40
✓ Total: 40¢

3. Make 75¢ with the fewest coins

  1. Use the biggest coin you can: quarters.
  2. 25, 50, 75: 3 quarters
✓ 7 dimes and 1 nickel also make 75¢, but that's 8 coins

4. Can I buy it?

Lemonade costs 45¢. You have 1 quarter and 2 dimes.

  1. Count: 25, 35, 45
  2. 45¢ is exactly enough. Yes!
✓ 45¢ can also be written $0.45
Watch out
  • A dime is smaller than a nickel but worth more (10¢ vs 5¢).
  • Count the biggest coins first so the counting is easier.
  • $0.75 and 75¢ are the same amount.
