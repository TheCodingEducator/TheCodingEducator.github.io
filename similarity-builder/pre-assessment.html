Answer Key: Pre-Assessment

Getting Ready for Similar Figures · for the teacher
  1. Any two of 2 : 3, 2 to 3, 2/3
  2. Table: lemons 2, 4, 8, 10; cups 3, 6, 12, 15
  3. × 5
  4. bigger; smaller; same
  5. 4 × 3 = 12 in
  6. 3 × 5 = 15 km
  7. A and B: every side of B is 1.5 times the matching side of A (2 × 1.5 = 3, 4 × 1.5 = 6). C is not: 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 but 4 ÷ 4 = 1.
  8. x = 7
  9. x = 20
  10. x = 8 (2 × 4 = 8, 3 × 4 = 12)

What each part tells you

PartWhy it matters for this unitIf many students miss it
A: RatiosSimilar figures are all about equal ratios between matching sides.Review ratio tables with Proportional Plates.
B: ScalingA scale factor is a multiplier: more than 1 enlarges, less than 1 shrinks.Enlarge and shrink a photo on a phone or projector and measure it.
C: Same shape?#7 is the test for similar rectangles: the same multiplier for every side.Draw rectangles on grid paper and compare them by multiplying, not adding.
D: EquationsMissing sides are found with equations like 3x = 21 and proportions like 2/3 = x/12.Review with Tip the Scales.

Reading the results: a student who says C matches A (“it’s only 1 wider”) is judging by looks or by adding, not multiplying; that's the key misconception this unit fixes, so address it on day one. Compare the “How sure do you feel?” circles with the answers: a student who circles “I've got it” but misses the part is worth a quick conversation.
