Similarity Builder

A runner game about similar figures. You run across a canyon, and at every gap a puzzle shows two similar shapes of different sizes: find the scale factor between them (level 1), or use the scale factor to find a missing side length by multiplying or dividing (levels 2 to 4, where the shapes get turned, flipped, and eventually only label one side out of each group of equal sides). Whatever number you answer is what the bridge gets built with - too small and it buckles, too large and it snaps, and either way you fall and lose a heart. Between puzzles, dodge obstacles that are the real-world reasons a bridge does not get built - protesters, endangered animals, lawyers who need permits, red tape, unstable soil, puddles, holes, loose pipes, and electrical lines. Each level has its own obstacles, and every one costs a heart if it touches you. Jump the ones on the ground and run under the ones overhead, and land on floating platforms. Every correct answer earns a coin to spend in the Shop on a bridge-building crew member such as a politician, architect, engineer, electrician, or safety inspector. Controls: Space, Enter, or the up arrow (or a tap) jumps, and pressing again in the air double jumps. Type whole-number answers and press Enter or the Build button. The Menu button or the Escape key pauses. From the title screen, Play opens the Menu screen, where you can start a Bridge Run, open Practice (no obstacles or hearts, with a choice of question types), or open the Shop.

📏 Common Core Standards

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.8.G.A.4

Understand that a two-dimensional figure is similar to another if the second can be obtained from the first by a sequence of rotations, reflections, translations, and dilations; given two similar two-dimensional figures, describe a sequence that exhibits the similarity between them.

Applies to: every level - the two shapes are always dilations of each other; levels 3-4 turn and flip the bigger shape.

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.7.G.A.1

Solve problems involving scale drawings of geometric figures, including computing actual lengths and areas from a scale drawing and reproducing a scale drawing at a different scale.

Applies to: level 1 (find the scale factor) and levels 2-4 (use it to find a missing length).

Teaching Notes for Educators

Similarity Builder is built around one skill: given two similar figures, find the scale factor and use it to find an unknown side length. Every gap in the canyon is one of these problems, and the answer the student types is literally the size the bridge gets built at - so the math decides whether the runner crosses or falls.

  • Four levels that build the skill step by step. Level 1 asks only for the scale factor between two dilated shapes (1 to 3, triangles and rectangles). Level 2 gives the factor and mixes multiplying and dividing to find a missing side (factors 2 to 4, seven shape types, small numbers) with the missing side spotlighted. Level 3 removes every hint - no arrows and no scale factor shown, just both shapes' side lengths with one missing - so students must find the factor themselves from a visible pair, on shapes that are turned and flipped, with round numbers (multiples of 5, nothing bigger than 100). Level 4 adds the reasoning step: only one number is shown for each group of equal sides, so students use tick marks and what they know about squares, rectangles, parallelograms, rhombuses, and isosceles and equilateral triangles to work out the sides that aren't labeled.
  • Correct sides are color-matched. Corresponding sides share a color across the two figures, including after the bigger figure is rotated or flipped, so students match sides by position in the figure instead of by where they happen to sit on the screen.
  • Whole numbers only. Every side length and scale factor is a whole number and the answer box only accepts whole numbers, keeping the focus on the proportional reasoning rather than on decimal or fraction arithmetic.
  • Right and wrong have a visible consequence. The bridge is built to the scale of the answer that was typed: a bridge that is too small buckles under the runner, one that is too large snaps in the middle, and either way the runner drops into the gap and loses a heart. It is a direct picture of what an incorrect scale factor does to a figure.
  • Mistakes become worked examples. After a wrong answer, the same two figures come back with every side labeled and a color-matched curved arrow on each side showing the multiplication (or division) by the scale factor - a picture of the calculation, not a wall of text.
  • An action game around the math. Between puzzles the student runs about ten seconds, using a double jump and floating platforms. Every 1000 m the runner speeds up and the obstacles get tighter, so success in the math is rewarded with a harder, faster run.
  • The obstacles are the real reasons bridges don't get built - and each level has its own. Instead of random hazards, students dodge what actually stands between a community and a new bridge, and every obstacle costs a heart if it touches you. Level 1 is simple things on the ground (boulders, cacti, logs, cones, an endangered tortoise). Level 2 adds taller and wider things (stumps, an endangered frog, hazmat barrels, red tape, "Private Property" fences) and the first flyer to run under. Level 3 brings wide hazards (puddles, holes, unstable soil), lawyers who want to see a permit, and low-hanging birds and electrical lines. Level 4 is big groups of protesters, loose underground pipes that burst up, and lots of overhead traffic (survey drones, an endangered falcon, storm clouds). Ground hazards are jumped, but overhead ones must be run under, so students read each obstacle rather than mashing the jump button. Spacing is irregular - tight clusters mixed with long breathers - and gets tighter each level, but there is always a way through (every gap leaves time to land before the next thing arrives). It opens an easy conversation about permits, the environment, safety, and why big projects take years.
  • Coins and a crew to hire. Every correct answer earns 1 coin (wrong answers never cost coins). Coins are spent in the in-game Shop to hire the bridge-building crew as the student's runner: a Politician (funding), Architect (design), Engineer (the math and forces), Electrician (power and signals), and Safety Inspector (safe to open) - each with their own look and a short note about what that job does on a real bridge project. The characters are cosmetic, so students choose them for fun rather than for an advantage.
  • Practice mode: pick exactly the skill you want. From the Menu screen, students (or a teacher choosing for them) can open Practice, a mode with no obstacles, no hearts, and no timer. They tick one or more question types - find the scale factor (level 1), find a side of the bigger shape, find a side of the smaller shape (level 2), the no-scale-factor turned-and-flipped questions (level 3), or the unlabeled-equal-sides questions (level 4) - and each type shows a plain-language description plus a live example. Questions then come from a random mix of the ticked types, wrong answers still show the worked example with the curved scale-factor arrows, and a summary card at the end shows accuracy and the missed questions. Students also earn coins for correct answers in Practice. Their selections are remembered on the device.
  • A title screen and a menu. The game opens on an animated title screen where a small figure is dilated into a larger similar one - dashed rays through matching corners and a "× 3" or "÷ 3" pill - while the runner hops obstacles. Play leads to the Menu screen, the one place to start a Bridge Run, open Practice, or visit the Crew Shop.
  • Timed, but forgiving. Each question has a countdown that tightens slightly on later levels, and a Hint button reminds students of the method without giving the answer. A Menu button (or the Esc key) pauses the whole game at any time, including mid-question.
  • Level checkpoints, so one bad run isn't a setback. A new level unlocks every 1000 m (level 2 at 1000 m, level 3 at 2000 m, level 4 at 3000 m). Each time a student reaches one, the game remembers the level and the distance. The Menu screen and the end-of-run screen both list every level with the distance a new run would start at - for example "Level 2 · 473 m" - with levels not yet reached shown locked. Picking one starts at the beginning of that level with the same distance and bridge count and three fresh hearts, instead of starting over from level 1. These checkpoints are saved in the student's own browser. That keeps students practicing at the level that challenges them rather than re-doing material they have already shown they can handle.
  • Low-stakes and replayable. No accounts and unlimited runs. Three hearts per run, a saved personal best distance, level checkpoints, coins, and the crew they have hired - all stored only in the student's own browser. Nothing is ever sent to a server, and there are no accounts, names, or other personal information, so the game is safe for students under 13 (COPPA).
