Visual Notes: Transformations of Figures

Grade 8 · CCSS 8.G.A.1, 8.G.A.2, 8.G.A.3 · Pairs with the Transformania game
Name

The big idea: a transformation moves a figure. A translation slides it, a rotation turns it around a point, and a reflection flips it across a line. None of them change its size or shape, so the image is always congruent to the pre-image.

Key vocabulary (dashed blue = before, the pre-image; orange = after, the image)

Translation

A slide. Every square moves the same number of units the same way. Here: 3 units right.

Rotation

A turn around a fixed point (red). Here: 90° clockwise, one quarter-turn.

Reflection

A flip across a line. Each square lands the same distance from the line, on the other side.

Sequence

Two or more in a stated order. Here: reflect across the line, then translate 1 unit right.

Turning on tracing paper

Trace the figure, put your pencil on the red point, and turn the paper. Every quarter-turn is 90°. Clockwise goes the way a clock’s hands move (an arrow pointing up turns to point right); counterclockwise goes the other way.

90°1 turn
180°2 turns
270°3 turns
360°4 turns (back where it started)

Worked examples

Rotate 180° counterclockwise.

180° ÷ 90° = 2 quarter-turns to the left. The arrow on the paper goes up → left → down.

Reflect across the horizontal line.

The square 1 unit above the line lands 1 unit below it; the one 3 above lands 3 below. Top and bottom swap.

Translate 2 left, then rotate 90° clockwise.

Order matters: slide first, then turn the slid figure around the red point.

Remember

  • Compare with the pre-image: pick one square and follow it to its new place.
  • Turns are checked for direction as well as amount: 180° clockwise and 180° counterclockwise land in the same place but turn opposite ways.
  • A vertical line goes up and down; a horizontal line goes side to side.
  • Translations, rotations and reflections keep lengths and angles the same: the image is congruent.
