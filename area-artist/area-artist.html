Area Artist

A multiplication art game. A hidden pixel-art picture is split into rectangles. The game zooms in on one rectangle at a time; find how many squares are in it by multiplying its rows by its columns (or find a missing side), and it fills in with its part of the picture. After each piece the game zooms out so you can see the picture growing. Product Picasso uses 1 by 1 up to 5 by 5 rectangles with every square drawn so it can be counted; Multiplication Monet uses 5 by 5 up to 12 by 12. In the art studio you design your own picture: choose two numbers, solve the multiplication, and place the rectangle anywhere. Finished pictures are kept in your gallery on this device.

Teaching Notes for Educators

Students multiply to reveal a hidden pixel-art picture. The picture is cut into rectangles, and each one fills in with its part of the artwork once its multiplication is solved. The game zooms in on a random piece, so the picture stays a mystery until near the end, and zooms back out after each piece to show how it's growing.

  • Two kinds of pieces: find the number of squares (3 × 4 = ?), or find a missing side (3 × ? = 12).
  • Product Picasso (easy): rectangles from 1 × 1 to 5 × 5, with every unit square drawn so students can always count to check.
  • Multiplication Monet (hard): rectangles from 5 × 5 to 12 × 12. The squares are hidden until a wrong answer, when they appear so the student can count or skip-count.
  • Wrong answers: the student is reminded that they can count each individual square, the squares are drawn on the rectangle, and they try again. The fill animation counts every square as it paints.
  • Picture size: every square of the picture is one solid color, so the picture is built from the squares students count. Short, medium and long pictures take roughly 35, 55 and 80 pieces in Product Picasso, and 10, 20 and 40 bigger pieces in Multiplication Monet.
  • Art studio: students pick rows and columns, solve the multiplication, and place the rectangle anywhere on the canvas. Rotating a rectangle (3 × 4 into 4 × 3) shows the commutative property. Creations are saved in the gallery on the device and can be downloaded.
  • Discussion: ask students to find two different rectangles with the same number of squares, or to explain how a 6 × 7 rectangle can be split into a 5 × 7 and a 1 × 7 (the distributive property).

Key vocabulary

  • area: the number of unit squares that cover a flat shape
  • unit square: a square with sides 1 unit long; it covers 1 square unit
  • square units: the unit area is measured in (12 square units)
  • row: a line of squares going across
  • column: a line of squares going up and down
  • array: objects arranged in equal rows and columns, like 3 rows of 4
  • side length: how many units long one side of a rectangle is
  • factor: a number being multiplied; in 3 × 4 = 12, the factors are 3 and 4
  • product: the answer to a multiplication; in 3 × 4 = 12, the product is 12
  • missing factor: the unknown number in 3 × ? = 12; divide to find it: 12 ÷ 3 = 4
