What each part tells you

PartWhy it matters for this unitIf many students miss it
A: Count the squaresArea is the number of unit squares that cover a shape.Cover rectangles with square tiles and count them.
B: Rows and columnsSeeing equal rows is the step from counting to multiplying.Arrange counters in rows; point to each row as you count.
C: Skip countingSkip counting by a row's length is the fastest way to find area before the facts are known.Daily skip-counting chants by 2s, 5s, 10s and 3s.
D: Equal groupsRepeated addition is the meaning of multiplication: 3 rows of 4 = 4 + 4 + 4 = 3 × 4.Write the addition sentence under every array before writing the multiplication.

Reading the results: students who count every square one by one on #1 and #2 are ready, but slow; skip counting by rows is the next step. Students who notice that every answer is 12 are already thinking like multipliers. Compare the “How sure do you feel?” circles with the answers: a student who circles “I've got it” but misses the part is worth a quick chat.
