Battle on the Coordinate Plane

A sea battle game on the coordinate plane. Place two ships on your board, then take turns with the computer. On your turn, type an ordered pair to fire; a plane flies from the origin, first left or right for x, then up or down for y. On the computer's turn, find its ordered pair on your own board and select it. Sink both enemy ships to win. Everything works with the keyboard: Tab between controls, arrow keys move around a board, Enter selects, R rotates a ship, Escape opens the menu.

📏 Common Core Standards

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.5.G.A.1

Use a pair of perpendicular number lines, called axes, to define a coordinate system, with the intersection of the lines (the origin) arranged to coincide with the 0 on each line. Understand that the first number indicates how far to travel from the origin in the direction of one axis, and the second number indicates how far to travel in the direction of the second axis.

Applies to: every shot. The plane flies from the origin along the x-axis first, then turns and flies parallel to the y-axis.

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.5.G.A.2

Represent real world and mathematical problems by graphing points in the first quadrant of the coordinate plane, and interpret coordinate values of points in the context of the situation.

Applies to: Easy mode (x and y from 0 to 6).

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.6.NS.C.6

Understand a rational number as a point on the number line. Extend number line diagrams and coordinate axes to represent points on the line and in the plane with negative number coordinates. (6.NS.C.6b: signs of numbers in ordered pairs indicate locations in quadrants. 6.NS.C.6c: find and position pairs of integers on a coordinate plane.)

Applies to: Standard and Hard modes (−3 to 3), quadrant challenges, and finding the computer's shots.

CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.6.NS.C.8

Solve real-world and mathematical problems by graphing points in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane.

Applies to: Standard and Hard modes.

Teaching Notes for Educators

A real sea battle against the computer, played like the classic ship-hunting board game, where every shot is an ordered pair. Students place two ships, fire by typing coordinates, and must find each of the computer's shots on their own board.

  • Three modes: Easy uses the first quadrant (0 to 6, origin in the bottom-left). Standard uses all four quadrants (−3 to 3, origin in the center). Hard (Coordinate Commander) uses all four quadrants with a timer, typed ordered pairs, and short Radar Check questions.
  • Ships sit on points, not in squares: Boat Buddy covers 3 points and Giant Galley covers 4, horizontal or vertical, and they can lie on an axis or across the origin.
  • The plane shows x first, then y: after every shot a plane starts at the origin, flies left or right for x, turns, then flies up or down for y, with the moves labeled ("2 right", "3 down").
  • Repeated shots are allowed on purpose: a student who keeps reversing x and y sees the plane land somewhere else and the miss stay on the board. The shot log lists every shot.
  • Finding the enemy's shots: the computer names a coordinate and the student must select it on their own board. Wrong picks get feedback about the specific mistake (x and y swapped, wrong direction for x or y) and stronger hints each time; on the fourth try the path is drawn on the board.
  • Hard mode: the computer's shots are given in words, often with y first ("The y-coordinate is −2 and the x-coordinate is 3", "Move 2 units down and 3 units right"). Students type full ordered pairs with parentheses and a comma. Radar Checks ask about quadrants, points on an axis, reading a highlighted point, and comparing points. Running out of time skips your shot, shows where the enemy's shot landed, or reveals the Radar Check answer.
  • Nothing is saved: no accounts, no scores kept, no data stored. Every game starts fresh.
